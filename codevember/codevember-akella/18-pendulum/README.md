# 18-pendulum

The day's page is the veil (`index.html`: a factory of cloth mirrors and a pendulum ball, with links across the top to three bigger balls); the other pendulum experiments are beside it, each an exact loop. All share `src/stage.ts` (studio room for reflections, overhead key
with shadows, soft mirror floor, bloom, grain).

| page | what | why it loops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `breather.html` (`src/breather.ts`) | 100 brass paddles on a spine of torsion springs, a sine-Gordon breather twisting the middle into a bundle and back | the breather is periodic in time, 2π/ω |
| `modes.html` (`src/modes.ts`) | a balanced five-branch mobile tied by springs; one branch plucked, the motion spreads and gathers back | M and K from exact kinematics, modes by Cholesky + Jacobi, each mode rounded to whole swings in the loop length that bends them least |
| `orbit.html` (`src/orbit.ts`) | double spherical pendulum on a relative periodic orbit; trails 12 periods long as lit tubes (glowing at the head, tarnishing towards the tail, shadowed on the floor) | the state comes back turned by Δφ; the eye turns with it, in a room the same all the way round. The trail is one period of tube instanced k times, each turned k·Δφ on the GPU, so any trail length loops |
| `triple.html` (`src/orbit.ts`) | the same for a triple spherical pendulum: three nested rosettes (orange cup, rose ring, blue skirt) | as above; orbits from `tools/chain-search.mjs` (any chain length, tensions by a tridiagonal solve), wild ones by continuation (`FROM=file.json:i` pumps the speeds up step by step and closes each again) |
| `window.html` (`src/window.ts`, `src/window-model.ts`) | a pendulum whose bob is a square window; inside it hangs one a third the size, and so on; the eye follows a window, then the one inside it, zooming ×3 per loop | the clock slows by √3 per zoom so the watched window keeps its pace; 7 levels simulated (each feels its parent's motion; lighter ones don't push back), starts shot at load so level j+1 ends with level j's starting angle and speed. Exact in position and speed; only the acceleration steps (1–3%) at the seam |
| `index.html` (`src/veil.ts`, `src/wrap-sim.ts`, `src/wrap.wgsl`; `veil.html` goes there) | after arc4g, a factory in a light studio: rose-gold cloth mirrors in frames ride a conveyor past a pendulum (just its cord, fading in from above, and its ball); the ball swings through the mirror in the station, takes the cloth away wrapped round it, turns back at the top of its swing and leaves it to fall, while the line indexes the next frame in with a clunk; the ball comes back through the new mirror from the other side | exact loop, one swing there and back (786 frames at the default slow motion; stills a loop apart are pixel-identical). Every cloth starts from the same settled sheet and meets the same arc (every other one turned round), on an integer frame clock, so every cloth lives the same life; the sim is bitwise repeatable (neighbour lists keep the nearest, sorted, and hash buckets never overflow). Each cloth sleeps (the settled sheet, drawn still) until 0.1 s before the hit, falls on one sweep with neighbour lists every 8 substeps from 1.5 s after it, and is retired (not simulated or drawn) once below the picture, 3.3 s after: the sim went from 13.0 to 7.5 ms a frame over a loop (headless, this Mac; the render is ~1.5 ms). Self-collision uses a counting sort by hash bucket (nothing dropped, so runs repeat bit for bit). The floor has a square hole on each side where the shed cloths drop (sized from where they actually fall: within 0.42 m of the line's axis and 1.98 to 2.73 m out), with shafts that go dark (the cloth's room light is occluded below the floor); soft key shadows, a cool rim, a pool of light on the floor, haze that takes the line's far ends into the backdrop, and a camera from above the incoming side so new frames appear out of the picture. Screen-space passes (`src/screen-space.ts`): reflections traced through the depth buffer for the frames, ball and floor (a metal shows what the ray finds, times its own colour, in place of the room; the floor adds a soft one); none from the cloth or the frames (they broke up on fine folds and thin bars): it shows the room and the ball and cord, traced in its shader. Occlusion at a quarter of the picture's size (each surface says in its normal's alpha how much it takes: none on the frames and fading out over the cloth's border, where the frame's lip made a thin contact line the quarter size broke into a dashed dirty band), also as interreflection on metal (p F / (1 - (1 - p) F)): copper seeing copper is what turns the inside of a wrapped fold orange, as in arc4g's cloth. The occlusion is N8AO (N8python's, CC0; the library is WebGL only, so `src/n8ao.ts` is its algorithm in TSL: hemisphere points checked against depth, then a plane-and-normal-aware Poisson blur twice, the second at twice the size so it is also the upsample; noise fixed to the screen so the loop stays exact), or three's GTAO. Reflections are off by default (the costliest part; `?on=ssr,blur` brings them back, `?on=gtao` starts on GTAO). The gui's performance folder shows fps and cloth sim GPU ms and switches each part while the page runs. Measured over a loop at 3024x1800 headless with two frames in flight (GPU-bound; the CPU is ~1 ms a frame): 7.8 ms a frame by default. The cloth sim 5.45 of it: self-collision 2.3 (rebuilding neighbour lists every 8 substeps, `?lists=4` for the old 4), the springs' two sweeps 1.8, the rest (predict, ball and rod, publishing to the textures) 1.35. The sheet is 224 particles a side (4.6 ms; 256 looked the same and cost 5.45, `?n=256`; `?n=192` 3.9, slightly smoother folds). The render without post ~1; bloom 0.4; N8AO 1.3 (0.5 + its denoise 0.8; GTAO 1 more); SSR at half size +1 (`?ssr=1` +2.6), on by default and turned off for good if the page can't keep 57 fps with it over two 2 s windows running (`?adaptive=0` never, `?skip=ssr` without; the performance folder shows what it decided, and a switch thrown there by hand ends it); the shadow map at 2048 (was 4096, -2.5 ms; `?shadowmap=4096`) and drawing the cloth cost about nothing measurable. The page draws 60 frames a second whatever the screen (the clock is whole frames: a 120 Hz screen played the loop twice as fast), not while recording; three's GPU timestamps are off unless `?gputime=1` (read back every frame they cost frames at 60 Hz, and read wrong). One sim with twice the particles costs more than two sims, so the sims are bound by their work, not by their many small dispatches: batching the cloths would not help. `?post=0` turns the passes off; `?ssdebug=1`/`2`/`5`/`6`/`7`/`8` shows the reflections/occlusion/normals/depth/what is not a finite number (red the colour, magenta the normal or a zero one, cyan the occlusion, green the reflectance, blue the composite; yellow brighter than 64)/which term of the composite is not finite. Nothing that is not a finite number gets past the passes (`src/finite.ts`): one NaN pixel on a crumpled cloth (a power of an occlusion a hair below 0) went through the bloom's smallest image as a black square over most of the screen. The bloom sees each pixel at most `bloomCap` (4) bright: one-pixel glints on the mirror folds near the ball, thousands of times brighter than the room, bloomed into squares that blinked as they moved. Shadows are variance shadow maps (soft; `?shadows=pcf` for the old ones). The links across the top, the gui's "ball size" (0.06 to 0.7 m) and `?ball=0.4` set the ball's radius. It changes as the page runs: the radius eases over 1.5 s, spread over the sim's substeps, and the cloths feel it; nothing restarts, and once the cloths that lived through it are gone the loop is exactly a fresh page's (stills identical). A bigger ball touches the sheet sooner, so its cloths wake sooner (`wakeFor`: 0.1 s before the hit at 0.16 m, 0.18 at 0.52); that schedule only changes while no frame is settling for its hit, so growing can wait up to 1.3 s. The picture is graded after the tone mapping (`tone`: neutral, agx or aces; `grade`: a gentle S curve, a touch more saturation, cool darks and warm lights, in the studio folder). `tools/film.mjs` shoots the loop (`SET='{"film":0.015,"fov":36}' DPR=2 node tools/film.mjs output/pendulum_x.mp4`: 393 frames, every second sim frame), the thumbnail is `?ball=0.38` at phase 0.39 from close up (distance 3.6, fov 24, target 0.06 0.14 -1.3). While it loads the page says "penduluming": the letters hang on an arc and a ball on a cord swings through them, each knocked and lit as it passes (CSS alone, transforms and opacity; a letter's moment is acos(-angle / amplitude) / 2π of the period), fading once the card has drawn the third frame. The resting sheet is settled two frames at a time at load: in one lump it was 1.4 s of GPU work in which nothing else reached the screen. Press C to take the view you've dragged to as the page's own and print it (for pasting into `look`). The frames stand 3.1 m apart and wobble on their carriages as the line moves them (wiggle bones, after the wiggle library: two bones, carriage to the frame's middle and middle to its top; each tip is a spring pulled to where it would rest on its parent, so the frame trails as it rides and sways past when it stops. They follow the carriage exactly as it is drawn, so nothing leans before it moves; the line's moves are a firm smoothstep (their push starts and ends at once) with a small clunk, so the frames lean back as they set off and sway in place when they stop; the lower bone is damped on its own speed (it trails), the upper more and against the lower (it bends, without a ringing of its own); worked out once on the CPU for frame 0's whole life, frame by frame, so every frame wobbles alike and the loop stays exact; bent in the vertex shader of the frame, its stem and the still cloth in it). The last move's sway rings on as the ball comes, so the slot in the top bar swings off the cord's line, and what is left fades out just before the cloth wakes: from then until the line moves on the frame is held still, as the cloth's sim needs. The rod is solid too and comes through a slot in each frame's top bar, where the cloth is not held. Low friction on the ball lets it shed the cloth on the way back; gravity 3 (real gravity pulls the cloth straight off the ball) |
| `veil-pass.html` (`src/veil-pass.ts`) | the single pass the machine came from: one cloth, one ball | XPBD 256² grid (2 sweeps, substeps of 0.5 ms whatever the playback speed), edges on stiff springs that let go past their own grip (spread, so the edge unzips), friction on the ball, self-collision through a spatial hash and per-particle neighbour lists, quadratic air drag on the faces |
| `soap.html` (`src/soap.ts`, `src/veil-model.ts`) | first take on the veil, as a soap film (the user wanted cloth instead): a ball meets its reflection in a chrome film, the film wraps it, seals behind it and heals; the ball flies on in a chrome skin | not a loop yet (restarts each cycle): one pass to get the physics right. Film = surface tension (Dziuk's semi-implicit scheme: cotan Laplacian of the current surface, banded Cholesky every 4 steps), log-polar mesh, the neck ring cut into two poles when it shrinks below the film's thickness |
| `edge.html` (`src/edge.ts`, `src/edge-model.ts`) | 622 pendulums with Harper-Hofstadter couplings (after Süsstrunk & Huber); a rim pluck splits into two helical edge waves | modes in the gap rounded to the lap time where their frequencies agree best |
| `spring.html` (`src/spring.ts`) | crown of resonant (2:1) swinging springs, bounce and swing trading places, the plane stepping a third of a turn | orbit shot to come back turned by exactly 120°, three exchanges a loop |
| `chimera.html` (`src/chimera.ts`) | two platforms of metronomes on a spring: one in step, one arguing | a choreography: Adler's closed form, whole slips a loop; platforms move by momentum |
